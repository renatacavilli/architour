Journey to Buenos Aires

With the support of the Col·legi d’Arquitectes de Catalunya (Architects’ Association of Catalonia), architour is organising an architects’ trip to the city of Buenos Aires.

The trip spans 14 days in total: 12 full days of guided architectural routes and activities, plus 2 extra days for the flights. The dates have been chosen to coincide with the 20th Buenos Aires Architecture Biennial as a complementary offer, with 4 afternoons left free for it.

Architour, with the Col·legi d’Arquitectes de Catalunya helping to spread the word, organises the travel group, with a minimum of 12 participants and a maximum of 30. Participants register in advance and confirm their commitment with a prepayment or deposit. Payment is made in three instalments. Information sessions will be held for those interested, where all the details will be explained.

Announcement by the Col·legi d’Arquitectes de Catalunya

More information:

The trip is planned for 16–29 October 2026 and includes:

  • Design, organisation and guiding of the architectural routes and activities, by architour
    • Guided architectural itineraries: 10 routes
    • A book (PDF) with the full documentation of the programme, including extensive research for each route: maps, thematic overview, a file on each building, historical images and bibliography
    • Optional activities, not included in the price: an architecture trip to Colonia del Sacramento, Uruguay.
  • Logistics, by El Corte Inglés (travel agency):
    • Return flight from Barcelona to Buenos Aires
    • 12 nights in a central 4-star hotel, in a double room with breakfast. The Hotel Savoy is a heritage building https://www.savoyhotel.com.ar/
    • Tourist tax
    • Airport–hotel and hotel–airport transfers
    • Travel insurance for the duration of the stay
    • Return trip to the city of La Plata (R4)

Not included: meals, travel within the city, supplement for a private room.

Why Buenos Aires:

For the founding and formation of the colonial chessboard city, which influenced Cerdà’s Eixample; for the development of the baroque city of monumental radiating axes, until it became the cosmopolitan contemporary metropolis that never sleeps.

For the complexity of the phenomenon of a modern city, and for its rich relationship, past and present, with the Río de la Plata.

For its fertile and extensive Art Deco and Art Nouveau architecture. For a vast and powerful rationalist architecture, the fruit of the interaction between immigrant European architects and local ones.

For the reach of a liberal model, understood as the moderniser of the old colonial signs, applied from the bare industrial architecture of the port docks and railway stations to the opulence of Beaux-Arts architecture and its urban domes.

For the skyscraper model, rooted here since 1930, and for the concrete technology that made it possible, as the representative typology of the urban bourgeoisie and the rental apartment building.

For the architectural and sociological interest of the casa chorizo, that hybrid half Roman domus turned conventillo, a model of rental housing.

For the lush, exquisite parks that need no irrigation, and for the enormous plane trees whose canopies weave the streets into green vaults.

For the giant natural elements: the bright blue skies and their dazzling palette; for the Río de la Plata, which seems more like a sea, with no end in sight.

For the cafés and the intellectual and bohemian life, and for the life and intensity of the grand avenues and the textured cobbled streets.

For the lobbies of the buildings and their entrance doors: from the magnificent eclectic or rationalist apartment blocks, always clad with granite bases and bronze joinery, to the intimate zaguán of the single-family casa criolla.

For the immersion in a varied and rich cultural and historical production.

For the phantasmagorical night of the illuminated signs of late-night cinemas and theatres. For the reflections and night lights of the glass office towers.

Crossing the Río de la Plata –the link of a culture that bears its name– is not to be missed, with the whole impressive Buenos Aires skyline revealing itself from the ferry. Devoting a day to the Uruguayan city of Colonia del Sacramento –with its rich urbanism and stark Portuguese architecture– is a great counterpoint, or complement, to Buenos Aires.

And also because it is the city where I was born and where I lived, which adds the perspective of closeness and the critical reflection of someone both Catalan and porteño.
